Guide

How Steward works

The first start

Steward runs on Android 10 or newer. It has no account and no sign in. Every trip you follow is watched live by a paid flight data service, so using Steward needs a subscription; there is no free version and no ads. New subscribers get 3 days free through Google Play.

Steward asks for notifications, so alerts can reach you. Other asks come only when you turn on the thing that needs them: the calendar for Look in my calendar, and a rough location for From where I am.

Four tabs

The bar at the foot of the screen has four tabs, Flights, Sky, Passport and Settings, and a round add button for a new flight. Read at the top, tap at the bottom: the big buttons of each screen sit in thumb reach.

Flights

Flights is the home screen. Your next trips are listed under Next trips, each with a countdown, the gate when the airport gives it, and its status.

Found in your calendar
With Look in my calendar on, flights Gmail put in your calendar wait here. Tap Add, or Not mine to hide one.
Travel day
On the day you fly, the next flight fills the top in big type: the gate (or Gate soon), when it leaves and the time to go, with Boarding pass and Share ETA buttons.
Get gate and delay alerts
This card shows when notifications are off. Turn on fixes it.
Your trips are not watched
Without a live subscription your trips stay here to read. See plans starts or restarts one.

Add a flight

Tap the add button. Type the flight number, like OU 410, and pick the day from the chips above the box (Later picks any day). Times are local to the departure airport.

Find in my calendar
Reads your calendar on the phone and lists the flights Gmail put there.
Share a booking
In Gmail or the airline app, tap Share and pick Steward. Steward finds the flight numbers and the date in the text and looks them up. Nothing else from the email is kept.
Read a boarding pass
A screenshot of the pass from your photos, or a PDF or Wallet pass from the airline email. Every leg is added with its seat, and the pass is kept with its flight.
A file from another app
A pass or booking another app shares or opens with Steward is listed first. Nothing is added, and no flight is counted, until you tap Add.

The flight screen

Tap a trip to open it: the flight on a world map with the plane on its route, then the terminal, gate, seat and plane, a timeline with the planned times struck through, and words like 9m early. In the air it shows the altitude, the speed and the distance to go.

Before you fly
Pass, Share and My plane at the foot. When to leave for the airport, Directions to open your maps app, and about when the doors close.
My plane
Opens the Your plane screen, below.
A photo of your plane
Tap the Plane box or the Aircraft row to see a photo of that very plane from Planespotters.net, with the photographer's name and a link to the photo page. Before the airline names the plane, the same sheet says when it will.
After landing
Welcome to the city, the local time and your body clock, the bag belt, and Share arrival with Return flight, or Next pass when you have a connection.
Connections
When your next flight leaves from where this one lands, a Connecting flight card shows it, on Flights and on the flight screen. Tap it to open the next leg; on the flight screen Back walks you back leg by leg. When time is short the card says Tight connection, or You may miss it.
At the bottom
Show the live update again, Stop sharing the live link, and Stop following this flight (tap twice).

The details card ends with Local time at each airport. Boarding and doors are our estimate. The airline's time always wins.

Your plane

Tap My plane on the flight screen. One screen, top to bottom:

How this flight usually does
How late the flight usually leaves and lands, and how often it was on time, 15 to 30 minutes, 30 to 60 minutes or over an hour late, from its flights in the last 90 days. With too few flights it says Not enough history for this flight yet.
Your plane
The photo of the plane with its registration, then the type, the airline, its age, the seats and the engines. Airlines name the plane about a day before; until then Steward shows when it starts looking.
The plane before yours
Most delays start with the plane before yours. From 25 hours out, Steward follows the plane that flies in before yours, shows its turnaround, and gives the delay risk when it lands with too little time.

The live update on the lock screen

From two hours before departure, one live update shows your flight: the gate, a countdown to boarding, take off or landing, and one bar for the whole trip. On Android 16 and newer it also sits in the status bar as a small chip, like B42, Board, +35 or Belt 7. The lock screen copy never shows your seat.

Its buttons are Boarding pass (Show pass from boarding time, when a pass is kept), Share ETA, or Next flight when you have a connection, and Unpin. Unpin or a swipe hides it for that flight; Show the live update again, on the flight screen, brings it back.

Alerts

Gate and terminal changes, delays of 15 minutes or more, take off and landing with the arrival gate, the bag belt, and your plane running late. Reminders tell you when to check in, when to leave for the airport, and when boarding starts. Settings, Alerts, groups them into four switches. Cancellations and diversions always come through; a cancelled flight shows the other nonstop flights on the route in the next day, ready to follow.

The boarding pass

Open Boarding pass on a flight and add the PDF or Wallet pass from the airline email, or a screenshot from the airline app. Steward reads the barcode and draws it again, sharp and at full brightness. The pass stays on this phone. With several legs, swipe for the others. Add to home screen makes a shortcut to your next pass.

At boarding time a tap on the live update opens the pass over the lock screen, without unlocking. Only the pass shows, nothing else, and only from 3 hours before departure until the plane leaves the gate. A swipe over the lock screen reaches only the pass for the flight you are at the airport for, never next week's. Turn this off in Settings, Boarding pass.

Passes are sealed on the phone with a key only this phone holds. The pass screen never shows in a screenshot, a screen recording or the recent apps picture.

Share ETA

Share ETA sends a live link to the people picking you up, with a card of the route, the times and the status that chats show as a preview. The page opens in any browser, with no app and no account. It says in one sentence where the flight is, draws the route with the plane on it, gives the gate and terminal and the arrival in the reader's own time, and keeps itself up to date. It shows the flight only, never your seat or your name, and it ends a few hours after you land. Tell them again when I land, in the share sheet, gives you a Send that I landed note on arrival. Stop sharing the live link, on the flight screen, ends it at once.

Sky

Sky shows a turning globe with the flights people follow or look up with Steward, from a month before take off to a week after landing. A plane sits on each route that should be in the air now, placed by its times, so it is close, not exact. Under them, small dots are the airline flights in the air right now, heard by volunteers' ADS-B receivers (FlyItalyADSB, CC BY-SA 4.0, and adsb.lol, ODbL 1.0), each with a short wake that shows its heading; military aircraft and aircraft whose owners asked for privacy are left out. Pinch, double tap or use the zoom buttons to come in close; up close the dots turn into planes, and tapping one shows its flight, airline, aircraft, height and speed while the globe follows it. Below are how many are in the air, on the map and the airports, then the flights in the air, landing soonest first; tap one to turn the globe to it. It shows no flight numbers and nothing about who follows a flight, and looking needs no plan.

Passport

Every flight you follow lands in Passport when it reaches the gate. Passport opens on a turning globe with the year's routes drawn in gold; drag to turn it. Below it: the kilometres and how many times around Earth, the flights, the hours in the air and the airports, flights per month, how many arrived on time (within 15 minutes of the timetable), and the list of flights. With more than one year, tap a year to switch.

On the home screen and in Quick Settings

Next flight widget
The countdown, gate and status of your next flight.
Boarding pass tile
A Quick Settings tile with your next pass, one tap away.
Shortcuts
Touch and hold the Steward icon for Boarding pass, Share ETA and Add flight.

Settings

Settings is the third tab: one list of short rows, each opening its own page. Back returns to the list where you left it.

Needs a fix
Shows only when Android holds something back, each with a Fix button: Notifications, Live updates on the lock screen (Android 16 and newer) and Exact timing (Android 12 and newer). When all is on, the list starts with Look.
Look
Match the phone, Dark or Light, and Sky colours, a faint tint of the hour on home. Dark is a calm graphite, with blue kept for what you can tap.
Your trips: Alerts
Four switches: Before you fly (check in, when to leave, boarding with your pass), Changes to your flight (a new gate, delays of 15 minutes or more, a late plane), Take off and landing (with the arrival gate and your bag belt), and Live on the lock screen. Cancellations and diversions always reach you.
Your trips: Leaving for the airport
Time to the airport, Be at the airport, and From where I am, a rough location on the day of travel that stays on the phone.
Your trips: Boarding pass
Show on the lock screen: at the airport, without unlocking.
Your trips: Calendar
Look in my calendar, and Keep flights in my calendar, which writes each flight with its latest times, terminal and gate. Gmail's own event keeps the booked times.
Your trips: Units
Temperature in °C or °F, for the weather where you land, and Distance in km or mi, on each flight and in Passport. Both start from your phone's region.

Without a connection

Everything Steward last heard stays on the phone: your flights, gates, times, the boarding pass, your plane and the weather where you land. When the phone is offline, or Steward cannot reach its server, a red note stays at the top of every screen with times, saying since when: "No connection since 14:05. Check the airport screens." It goes away by itself once Steward hears from its server again.

The subscription

Steward is free to download, and using it needs a subscription. Every plan has every feature and covers every phone you have; plans differ only in how many flights you follow a month.

Steward
10 flights a month. 4.99 a month or 29.99 a year.
Pro
30 flights a month. 9.99 a month or 59.99 a year.
Unlimited
Unlimited flights. 19.99 a month or 99.99 a year. Meant for personal and family use, about 200 trips followed at once.

Prices are the listed ones in US dollars; Google Play shows your own price before you pay. New subscribers get 3 days free, one trial per Google account. After the trial the subscription starts unless you cancel in Google Play before it ends. A flight counts when you follow it. A connection on the same trip is free (a flight leaving from where your last one landed, within 24 hours), and so is a flight another of your phones already follows. A flight removed within an hour is given back, and the count starts again on the 1st. On Steward and Pro, 10 more flights cost 1.99 and never run out. If the subscription ends, your trips stay in the app as a read only list.

Privacy

No ads, no tracking, no analytics and no sign in. To watch a flight, Steward sends its server the flight number, the day and the seat you added, and a push token so alerts can reach your phone. Your calendar is read on the phone, and only the flight number and day of a flight you add leave it. Boarding passes stay on your phone, sealed with a key only it holds. From where I am uses a rough location on the day of travel, kept on this phone. A plane photo is looked up by the plane's registration only, and only when you open it. The full privacy policy says what is kept and for how long.

Accessibility

Light and dark looks, big text and TalkBack labels. Buttons are at least 48 dp, and travel day mode reads as one sentence with TalkBack.
